What to do when you blank on test day

It happened to me. A few slow breaths got me back.

4 min read

I blanked on test day. My mind just stopped working for a moment. So I stopped, took several deep breaths, and got back into the flow.

That's the whole story, and I'm telling it because blanking isn't a sign you're unprepared. It happens to well-prepared people. What matters is having a reset ready so it costs you thirty seconds instead of a whole module.

What blanking actually is

When you're stressed, your body reacts as if something is physically wrong. Your heart speeds up, your breathing gets shallow, and it gets harder to hold information in your head. That's the blank: not a lack of knowledge, but a temporary loss of access to it.

The good news is that breathing is one of the few parts of that reaction you can control directly. Slowing it down tells the rest of your body to calm down too.

The reset

  1. Notice it. The moment you realize you're rereading the same sentence without taking it in, stop trying to push through. Pushing makes it worse.
  2. Look away from the question. Take your eyes off the screen for a moment.
  3. Take several slow, deep breaths. Breathe in slowly, and let each breath out a little slower than you took it in.
  4. Come back to something easy. If the question you're on is the problem, use Mark for Review and move to the next one. Getting one question right is often what gets you back into rhythm.

Thirty seconds of breathing costs you far less than five minutes of panicked rereading.

Bluebook tools that help

  • Mark for Review lets you flag a question and come back to it at the end of the module. Skipping a hard question isn't giving up — it's protecting your time for the ones you can get.
  • The timer can be hidden. If watching the clock is feeding the panic, hide it for a while.
  • The answer eliminator lets you cross out choices. When your head is foggy, crossing out one wrong answer is a small, concrete win that gets you moving again.

Practice the reset before you need it

Don't wait for the real test to try this. Next time you get stuck or frustrated during a practice test, run the reset on purpose. The more times you've done it, the more automatic it becomes, and the less scary blanking is when it happens for real.
