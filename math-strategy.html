My math strategy: learn it once, then solve a lot of questions

No formula memorization. Learn the concept, learn the Desmos shortcut, then do question after question.

6 min read

My belief is that you shouldn't memorize formulas for SAT Math. The approach is simpler: understand each concept once, then solve as many questions as possible on that type until it was automatic.

Memorizing a formula tells you what to write down. It doesn't tell you when to use it. That recognition only comes from seeing a question type enough times that you know what it wants before you finish reading it. And the test gives you a reference sheet anyway — the formulas are right there.

Step 1: Learn the concept

For each topic, watch one good explanation until the idea makes sense. Not five videos — one, understood properly. These are the ones I recommend by topic:

Algebra

  • Linear equations in one variable
  • Linear equations in two variables
  • Systems of linear equations and inequalities
  • Linear inequalities: video 1, video 2

Geometry and trigonometry

  • Area and volume: video 1, video 2
  • Lines, angles, and triangles
  • Right triangles and trigonometry
  • Circles

Step 2: Learn Desmos

Bluebook has a Desmos graphing calculator built into the Math section, and it's one of the biggest advantages you have on the digital SAT. Plenty of questions that take several lines of algebra can be solved by graphing and clicking. James Lu's Desmos videos on YouTube are what I used to learn it.

A few moves that come up constantly:

  • Systems of equations: type both equations in and click the intersection.
  • Quadratics: graph the function and click the vertex or the x-intercepts.
  • Checking answers: when you're unsure, graph your answer and see if it matches the question.

Desmos doesn't replace understanding the math — you still need to know what the question is asking for. But it turns a lot of slow algebra into a few seconds of clicking. Practice with it in Bluebook before test day so you're fast with it.

Step 3: Solve a lot of questions

This is where most of the time goes. After learning a concept, drill that specific question type on Khan Academy until you stop missing it. Then mix types together, because the real test won't tell you which concept a question uses.

And review every miss using the four categories. In math especially, a lot of mistakes that feel like content gaps turn out to be misreads — you solved correctly for the wrong thing.

Try it

Both of these can be solved by hand or in Desmos. Try both ways.

Systems of equationsTry it
3x + 2y = 16
x − y = 2

In the system of equations above, what is the value of x?

By hand: from the second equation, x = y + 2. Substitute into the first: 3(y + 2) + 2y = 16, so 5y = 10 and y = 2. That makes x = 4.

With Desmos: type both equations in and click where the lines cross. The point is (4, 2), so x = 4.

A is the trap: 2 is the value of y. Whether you solve by hand or in Desmos, always check which variable the question asks for.

QuadraticsTry it
f(x) = x² − 6x + 5

What is the minimum value of the function f defined above?

With Desmos: graph it and click the lowest point of the parabola. It's (3, −4). The minimum value is the y-coordinate: −4.

C is the trap — 3 is where the minimum happens, not what it is. B and D come from the x-intercepts (1 and 5), which are where f(x) = 0.
