The review: turn every mistake into a lesson

Taking practice tests shows you your score. Reviewing them is what changes it.

6 min read

I tracked my mistakes by reviewing them in Bluebook. No elaborate spreadsheet, no color-coded notebook. After a practice test, I went back through what I got wrong.

That sounds almost too simple, but it's the part most people skip. They finish a practice test, check the score, feel good or bad about it, and start the next one. The score tells you where you are. The review tells you what to do about it.

Where to find your review

When you finish a Bluebook practice test, your results go to My Practice at mypractice.collegeboard.org. Sign in with the same account you use for Bluebook, then open Score Details.

That page lists every question on the test, with your answer, the correct answer, and a Review button that explains each answer choice — including why the wrong ones are wrong. That last part is the most useful thing on the page.

How to review a question

For every question you got wrong, go through these four steps:

  1. Redo it before reading the explanation. Sometimes you'll get it right the second time, which tells you the problem was pressure or timing, not understanding.
  2. Read the explanation for every choice. Not just the correct one. Understanding why your answer was wrong is the actual lesson.
  3. Name why you missed it. Put it in one of the four categories below.
  4. Note the question type. Central Ideas, Words in Context, linear equations, circles — whatever it was.

Also review questions you got right by guessing. A lucky guess is a mistake that happened not to cost you points this time.

The four reasons you miss a question

  • Content gap. You didn't know the rule, concept, or formula. Fix: learn it, then drill that question type on Khan Academy.
  • Misread. You knew how to solve it but read the question or passage wrong. Fix: slow down on the reading, not the solving.
  • Trap. You fell for an answer designed to look right. Fix: learn the method for that question type — the traps are predictable once you know them.
  • Time. You rushed or ran out of time. Fix: practice pacing on full-length tests, and skip and come back instead of getting stuck.

Each category has a completely different fix. That's the whole point of labeling your mistakes.

Which category does this mistake belong in?

This is the true but useless trap from the Command of Evidence post. Your answer was accurate, so you read the graph fine — that rules out A.

Here's why labeling matters: the fix for a trap isn't more content study. It's a method fix. In this case, write your test idea down before you look at the choices. If you'd filed this under "content gap," you'd have spent hours studying graphs you already understand.

Finding the pattern

After a few tests, look back at your mistakes together. You'll almost always see a cluster: most of your misses fall into one or two question types, or one category keeps showing up.

That cluster is what you study next. Not the whole test — just the part that's actually costing you points. My Practice can help here too: from your results, the Practice Specific Questions link builds a set of questions based on how you did.
